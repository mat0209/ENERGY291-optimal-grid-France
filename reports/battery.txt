
Battery storage modeling — seasonal storage with representative days
   The battery storage model is based on the two-layer state-of-charge (SOC)
   decomposition proposed by Kotzur et al. (2018). This formulation enables
   seasonal storage to be captured correctly even when the year is represented
   by a small number of typical periods (here 10 representative days), which is
   the key limitation of the classical daily-cyclic battery model.

   Classical approach (limitation):
   In the standard formulation, the battery SOC is reset at the start of each
   typical day, i.e. the battery cannot carry energy from one representative day
   to the next. This implicitly assumes short-term (intra-day) storage only and
   underestimates the value of storage in systems with seasonal imbalances between
   renewable supply and demand.

   Kotzur decomposition:
   The total SOC at actual day i, hour t is decomposed as:

     SOC(r, i, t) = e_inter(r, i) + e_intra(r, d=f(i), t)

   where:
     - e_intra(r, d, t) is the intra-period SOC accumulated within typical day d,
       starting from zero at the beginning of each typical day. It captures the
       within-day charging and discharging profile and can be negative (net
       discharge since start of day).
     - e_inter(r, i) is the inter-period SOC at the start of actual day i. It
       accumulates the net daily SOC changes across the 366 actual days of the
       year, enabling energy to be carried from one period to the next.
     - f(i) is the typical day assigned to actual day i (from k-medoids clustering).

   Intra-period evolution (for each typical day d, hour t):
     e_intra(r, d, t) = e_intra(r, d, t-1) + b_charge(r,d,t) - b_discharge(r,d,t)
     e_intra(r, d, 0) = 0

   Inter-period evolution (for each actual day i):
     e_inter(r, i+1) = e_inter(r, i) + e_intra(r, f(i), T)

   Cyclic annual condition:
   For the model to represent a steady-state annual operation (i.e. the battery
   ends the year in the same state as it started), the following condition is
   enforced:

     sum_d [ cluster_size(d) × e_intra(r, d, T) ] = 0   for all r

   This is equivalent to e_inter(r, N+1) = e_inter(r, 1) and prevents the
   optimizer from artificially exploiting a one-time charge or discharge over
   the year.

   Capacity constraints (Appendix B simplification):
   Rather than enforcing the SOC bounds 0 ≤ e_inter(r,i) + e_intra(r,f(i),t) ≤ E_bat(r)
   for all N × T combinations (366 × 24 = 8 784 per region), auxiliary variables
   e_intra_max(r,d) and e_intra_min(r,d) track the maximum and minimum intra-period
   SOC within each typical day:

     e_intra(r, d, t) ≤ e_intra_max(r, d)   for all t
     e_intra(r, d, t) ≥ e_intra_min(r, d)   for all t

   The capacity constraints then reduce to N constraints per region:

     e_inter(r, i) + e_intra_max(r, f(i)) ≤ E_bat(r)   for all i   [upper bound]
     e_inter(r, i) + e_intra_min(r, f(i)) ≥ 0           for all i   [lower bound]

   This reduces the number of capacity constraints by a factor of T = 24.
