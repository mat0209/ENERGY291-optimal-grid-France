METHODOLOGICAL NOTES
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Inter-regional distance — tortuosity factor
   The column Distance_km in capacites_interregionales.csv is the Haversine
   (great-circle) distance between region centroids. The column
   Distance_km_adjusted applies a tortuosity factor of 1.25 to account for the
   fact that real transmission lines cannot follow straight-line routes (terrain,
   urban areas, existing right-of-way constraints).

   The factor 1.25 is taken from:
     - Hagspiel S. et al. (2014). "Cost-optimal power system extension under
       flow-based market coupling." Energy, 66, 654–666.
     - Fürsch M. et al. (2013). "The role of grid extensions in a cost-optimal
       transformation of the European electricity system until 2050."
       Applied Energy, 104, 642–652.
   Both studies use a detour factor of 1.25 for European transmission line cost
   estimation. Distance_km_adjusted is the recommended input for any cost
   calculations involving line length (e.g., investment cost per km).

Inter-regional transmission capacity — kV to MW conversion
   The ODRE dataset records each line by voltage class (400kV or 225kV) and type
   (overhead / underground) but does not include per-circuit power ratings.
   Thermal capacity in MW is assigned per circuit using the standard conductor
   parameters from the PyPSA-Eur line-type library (docs.pypsa.org/components/
   line-types), which follow ENTSO-E transmission standards:

     Voltage  Type         Conductor type              i_nom    S_nom (calc.)   Used
     ───────  ──────────   ─────────────────────────   ──────   ─────────────   ──────
     400 kV   Overhead     Al/St 240/40 4-bundle       2.58 kA  √3×400×2.58     1500 MW
                                                                ≈ 1787 MVA
     400 kV   Underground  —                            —       derated ~33 %   1000 MW
     225 kV   Overhead     Al/St 240/40 2-bundle       1.29 kA  √3×225×1.29     400 MW
                                                                ≈ 502 MVA
     225 kV   Underground  —                            —       derated ~40 %   300 MW

   The overhead MW values are the apparent power S_nom converted to active power
   at a typical operational power factor (~0.85 for 400 kV, ~0.80 for 225 kV),
   then rounded to the nearest 100 MW — consistent with the capacity assumptions
   used in PyPSA-Eur (Horsch et al., 2018) and corroborated by published data on
   specific French 400 kV circuits (e.g., Avelin–Gavrelle: 1500 MW per circuit).
   Underground cables are derated relative to overhead lines of the same voltage
   because buried cables dissipate heat less efficiently; the ~30–40 % reduction
   applied here is in line with standard practice in European grid-expansion studies
   (see e.g. Fürsch et al., 2013, Applied Energy 104, 642–652).

   Reference:
     Horsch J., Hofmann F., Schlachtberger D., Brown T. (2018).
     "PyPSA-Eur: An Open Optimisation Model of the European Transmission System."
     Energy Strategy Reviews, 22, 207–215.
     https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esr.2018.10.002
   Line-type table: https://docs.pypsa.org/v1.0.5/user-guide/components/line-types/

New overhead transmission line investment costs
   Only overhead (aerial) lines are considered for new construction. The RTE
   Schéma Décennal de Développement du Réseau (SDDR 2025, Fiche 13) confirms
   that underground AC cables at 400 kV are not a viable option in France (they
   would require HVDC conversion, adding 40–70 Md€), and that underground 225 kV
   cables cost on average 65 % more than overhead lines of comparable capacity —
   making overhead lines the economically preferred option for new transmission 
   corridors.

   Investment costs used in the model (overhead lines only):

     Voltage   Cost              Source
     ───────   ───────────────   ───────────────────────────────────────────────
     400 kV    750 EUR/MW/km     Danish Energy Agency, Technology Data for
                                 Energy Transport (July 2025), via PyPSA/
                                 technology-data (costs_2030.csv, HVAC overhead)
     225 kV    1 250 EUR/MW/km   Derived from RTE SDDR 2025, Fiche 13: the
                                 Sarry–Vielmoulin 225 kV rehabilitation (60 km
                                 aerial, ~30 M€) implies ~0.5 M€/km, i.e.
                                 ~1 250 EUR/MW/km at 400 MW capacity. New-build
                                 costs are consistent with this order of
                                 magnitude; no single-figure unit cost for new
                                 construction is published by RTE.

   Investment cost for a new corridor (i, j) is computed as:
     C_ij = cap_MW × Distance_km_adjusted × cost_EUR_per_MW_per_km
   where Distance_km_adjusted already incorporates the 1.25 tortuosity factor.

   References:
     Danish Energy Agency (2025). Technology Data for Energy Transport.
       https://github.com/PyPSA/technology-data (costs_2030.csv)
     RTE (2025). Schéma Décennal de Développement du Réseau — Fiche 13 :
       Choix technologiques.
       https://assets.rte-france.com/prod/public/2025-03/2025-03-21-sddr-fiche-13-choix-technologiques.pdf

K-medoids clustering — representative days
   10 representative days are selected from 2024 using k-medoids (PAM) on the
   full year without monthly constraint. The distance metric is a weighted
   Euclidean distance: 50% on demand profile, 50% on generation mix profiles
   (weighted proportionally to each technology's average share). Each
   representative day carries a weight equal to its cluster size / 366, used
   as a time-weight in the optimization objective.
   Source: src/clustering/kmedoids_2.py
   Output: data/final/new_representative_days.csv
           data/final/new_representative_day_profiles.csv
           data/final/day_cluster_assignment.csv

Battery storage modeling — seasonal storage with representative days
   The battery storage model is based on the two-layer state-of-charge (SOC)
   decomposition proposed by Kotzur et al. (2018). This formulation enables
   seasonal storage to be captured correctly even when the year is represented
   by a small number of typical periods (here 10 representative days), which is
   the key limitation of the classical daily-cyclic battery model.

   Classical approach (limitation):
   In the standard formulation, the battery SOC is reset at the start of each
   typical day, i.e. the battery cannot carry energy from one representative day
   to the next. This implicitly assumes short-term (intra-day) storage only and
   underestimates the value of storage in systems with seasonal imbalances between
   renewable supply and demand.

   Kotzur decomposition:
   The total SOC at actual day i, hour t is decomposed as:

     SOC(r, i, t) = e_inter(r, i) + e_intra(r, d=f(i), t)

   where:
     - e_intra(r, d, t) is the intra-period SOC accumulated within typical day d,
       starting from zero at the beginning of each typical day. It captures the
       within-day charging and discharging profile and can be negative (net
       discharge since start of day).
     - e_inter(r, i) is the inter-period SOC at the start of actual day i. It
       accumulates the net daily SOC changes across the 366 actual days of the
       year, enabling energy to be carried from one period to the next.
     - f(i) is the typical day assigned to actual day i (from k-medoids clustering).

   Intra-period evolution (for each typical day d, hour t):
     e_intra(r, d, t) = e_intra(r, d, t-1) + b_charge(r,d,t) - b_discharge(r,d,t)
     e_intra(r, d, 0) = 0

   Inter-period evolution (for each actual day i):
     e_inter(r, i+1) = e_inter(r, i) + e_intra(r, f(i), T)

   Cyclic annual condition:
   For the model to represent a steady-state annual operation (i.e. the battery
   ends the year in the same state as it started), the following condition is
   enforced:

     sum_d [ cluster_size(d) × e_intra(r, d, T) ] = 0   for all r

   This is equivalent to e_inter(r, N+1) = e_inter(r, 1) and prevents the
   optimizer from artificially exploiting a one-time charge or discharge over
   the year.

   Capacity constraints (Appendix B simplification):
   Rather than enforcing the SOC bounds 0 ≤ e_inter(r,i) + e_intra(r,f(i),t) ≤ E_bat(r)
   for all N × T combinations (366 × 24 = 8 784 per region), auxiliary variables
   e_intra_max(r,d) and e_intra_min(r,d) track the maximum and minimum intra-period
   SOC within each typical day:

     e_intra(r, d, t) ≤ e_intra_max(r, d)   for all t
     e_intra(r, d, t) ≥ e_intra_min(r, d)   for all t

   The capacity constraints then reduce to N constraints per region:

     e_inter(r, i) + e_intra_max(r, f(i)) ≤ E_bat(r)   for all i   [upper bound]
     e_inter(r, i) + e_intra_min(r, f(i)) ≥ 0           for all i   [lower bound]

   This reduces the number of capacity constraints by a factor of T = 24.

   Reference:
     Kotzur L., Markewitz P., Robinius M., Stolten D. (2018).
     "Time series aggregation for energy system design: Modeling seasonal storage."
     arXiv:1710.07593v2 [math.OC].
     https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.07593
